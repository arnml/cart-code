\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) reduces hallucination by grounding large language models in retrieved evidence, but deployed pipelines often retrieve a fixed paragraph budget regardless of query difficulty, increasing token cost without reliable quality gains. This work evaluates whether a training-free filter can compress retrieved context without degrading answer quality on distractor-heavy multi-hop benchmarks, where answers require combining evidence across paragraphs, and characterises why three alternative selectors underperform. Using matched samples and a fixed generator, we compare four fixed-budget baselines, similarity-gap truncation, a title-arm bandit, a contextual bandit, and Noise-Gate, which applies a cosine-similarity threshold plus Jaccard-overlap redundancy filtering over precomputed paragraph embeddings. On the reference benchmark, Noise-Gate preserves answer quality with 26.7\% fewer tokens than the full-retrieval baseline, while no alternative matches that trade-off. Two transfer pilots show that the same threshold scheme matches or exceeds the full-retrieval baseline with 22.1\% and 11.2\% fewer tokens, using at most one threshold step of recalibration. Comparator failures trace to topically adjacent BM25 distractors violating embedding-rank monotonicity, sparse title vocabulary across splits, and lexically matched features a linear reward model cannot separate.

\keywords{Retrieval-Augmented Generation \and Test-time Compression \and Context Filtering \and Noise Gating}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) reduces hallucination in large language models by grounding generation in retrieved evidence~\cite{lewis2020rag}. Candidate evidence is obtained through sparse term-frequency methods such as BM25, dense retrievers~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr}, or hybrids~\cite{zhao2026aigcsurvey}, then passed as context to the model. With a sufficiently capable generator, the quality bottleneck shifts from generation to context selection: which evidence to pass given a fixed retrieval upstream is the focus of this work.

Yet most retrieval systems return a fixed number of documents regardless of the query~\cite{klesel2025rag}. Fixed-$k$ persists for operational rather than empirical reasons: training a per-domain selector is expensive in supervision and compute, deployment infrastructure favours a single deterministic budget, and a fixed integer is easy to monitor. The cost is concrete. On HotpotQA distractor~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa} with the configuration of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, $k{=}10$ reaches token-level F1 0.792 at 1452.5 mean tokens per query, against 103.5 mean tokens at F1 0.387 with no retrieval, a roughly fourteenfold cost increase for a quality lift that does not always materialise on harder examples~\cite{verma2026reflectiverag,li2025bestpractices}.

Four families of alternatives to fixed-$k$ have been explored. Training-based adaptive retrieval~\cite{asai2024selfrag,guo2025dior,su2024dragin} learns when and how much to retrieve through extra supervision or model modification, a deployment cost where labelled data is scarce. Multi-agent and iterative filtering~\cite{chang2024mainrag,nahid2025prism} is training-free but routes each query through several language-model calls. Compression-based methods~\cite{jeong2025ecorag,singh2025chunkrag,xiong2025drrag} require additional infrastructure for chunking, evidentiality scoring, or classifier-based selection. Gap-based truncation~\cite{taguchi2025adaptivek} is single-pass and training-free but rests on an embedding-rank-monotonicity assumption that distractor-heavy benchmarks violate. The three comparators evaluated here --- gap-based truncation~\cite{taguchi2025adaptivek}, a title-arm bandit~\cite{auer2002ucb1}, and a contextual bandit~\cite{chu2011linucb} --- form a progression of increasing structure: Adaptive-$k$ is training-free, while the two bandits are learned selectors trained on reward statistics from a training split.

The problem is harder on multi-hop, distractor-heavy data. Multi-hop questions require linking evidence across more than one paragraph rather than extracting the answer from a single passage. Three structural properties of these benchmarks resist naive filtering. BM25-retrieved distractors are topically adjacent to gold evidence and close in embedding space, so methods that rank by embedding similarity cannot easily separate them~\cite{chiang2024plausible}. Multi-hop reasoning requires evidence sets that are jointly complementary rather than individually relevant, complicating per-document scoring~\cite{trivedi2023interleave}. The fixed-pool format of HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA~\cite{ho-etal-2020-constructing}, and MuSiQue-Ans~\cite{trivedi-etal-2022-musique} reframes selection as structured filtering over a small candidate pool rather than open-domain retrieval. Prior work indicates that these structures make selection unusually sensitive to candidate-pool design~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa,chiang2024plausible,trivedi2023interleave,ho-etal-2020-constructing,trivedi-etal-2022-musique}; Section~\ref{sec:analysis} quantifies how this sensitivity affects the selectors evaluated here.

A training-free filter exploiting dense semantic similarity has not been systematically evaluated against learned selectors on distractor-heavy multi-hop QA. This work introduces Noise-Gate, a training-free two-stage filter combining cosine-similarity thresholding with Jaccard-overlap redundancy removal; the redundancy stage is conceptually related to diversity-based reranking such as MMR~\cite{goldstein1998mmr}. The evaluation asks whether a training-free filter can compress context without quality loss on distractor-heavy benchmarks, why learned selectors fail on this class, and whether threshold settings transfer across datasets. The contributions are threefold. First, Noise-Gate is introduced and characterised as a training-free filter, with a $-26.7\%$ token reduction at F1 0.764 against 0.792 at $k{=}10$, validated by ablations and transferred to two further benchmarks; the reduction scales linearly with deployment volume. Second, a structural failure analysis ties each comparator's design hypothesis to the benchmark property it violates. Third, a matched-sample comparison of five strategies under a single generation model and prompt provides a controlled reference point for future work.

The paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:related} situates Noise-Gate within prior work on RAG, multi-hop distractors, and cost-aware evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:problem} formalises the cost-aware objective. Section~\ref{sec:methods} defines the five strategies. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports the main, transfer, and ablation experiments. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} characterises the failure modes of the comparators. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the Pareto position, cost, limitations, and future work. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

This paper connects three lines of work: evidence-grounded RAG, multi-hop question answering with structured distractors, and cost-aware RAG evaluation. Evidence-grounded RAG uses retrieval to reduce hallucination, making context selection a quality-critical step~\cite{lewis2020rag}. Multi-hop distractor benchmarks test selection over small fixed pools in which BM25 distractors are plausible and often close to gold evidence in embedding space~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa,chiang2024plausible,trivedi2023interleave}. Cost-aware RAG treats token use as an evaluation axis alongside quality, motivating a fixed generator and mean tokens per query~\cite{klesel2025rag,li2025bestpractices}. The selector families below position Noise-Gate against representative work across these areas.

\paragraph{Fixed-budget retrieval.} The canonical RAG formulation passes a fixed top-$k$ retrieved set to a generator~\cite{lewis2020rag}, and a recent survey of deployment patterns indicates that fixed top-$k$ remains the deployed default~\cite{klesel2025rag}. The present work treats fixed $k{=}10$ as a quality reference and evaluates whether comparable quality is reachable at lower token cost on the same fixed pool.

\paragraph{Training-based adaptive retrieval.} Self-RAG~\cite{asai2024selfrag}, DioR~\cite{guo2025dior}, and DRAGIN~\cite{su2024dragin} learn when to retrieve and how much to retrieve through additional supervision or model modification. The supervision and retraining required for new domains constitute a deployment cost that the present work seeks to avoid.

\paragraph{Multi-agent and iterative filtering.} MAIN-RAG~\cite{chang2024mainrag} and PRISM~\cite{nahid2025prism} are training-free, but each query passes through several language-model calls, which raises inference cost in proportion to the number of agents or iterations~\cite{li2025bestpractices}.

\paragraph{Compression-based methods.} ECoRAG~\cite{jeong2025ecorag} compresses retrieved documents through evidentiality-guided selection, ChunkRAG~\cite{singh2025chunkrag} filters at the chunk level via LLM-driven chunk scoring, DR-RAG~\cite{xiong2025drrag} adds a classifier-guided relevance step, and Adaptive-$k$~\cite{taguchi2025adaptivek} truncates the retrieved list at the largest similarity gap. These approaches differ in granularity (chunk versus paragraph), in supervision requirement (classifier training versus none), and in the assumption made about embedding-similarity rank. Among them, Adaptive-$k$ is the closest comparator to Noise-Gate at the design level, because both operate on cosine similarities of precomputed embeddings without per-domain training; the two differ in whether selection follows a single largest-gap cut or a per-paragraph threshold.

\paragraph{Bandits, set selection, and reranking.} UCB1~\cite{auer2002ucb1}, Thompson Sampling~\cite{thompson1933}, and contextual variants such as LinUCB~\cite{chu2011linucb} are classical decision-making algorithms for sequential allocation under uncertainty; the present work uses them as comparators rather than as proposed selectors. SetR~\cite{lee2025setr} and RankRAG~\cite{yu2024rankrag} reframe selection as set construction by accounting for between-document complementarity. The redundancy-removal stage of Noise-Gate is conceptually related to Maximum Marginal Relevance~\cite{goldstein1998mmr}, which trades off relevance and diversity over a candidate pool.

Noise-Gate differs from these families by operating at paragraph granularity without training, using dense semantic similarity for selection and redundancy removal for compression.

\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

This section formalises the cost-aware context-selection problem. Let $q$ denote the question and $D=(d_1,\dots,d_n)$ the candidate context, exposed as an ordered list of titled paragraphs. Retrieval is treated as fixed: $D$ is produced upstream and not under control. A context-selection strategy returns an ordered subsequence $S\preceq D$ concatenated with $q$ and passed to a generator $g$. The decision space is the set of subsequences of $D$.

Let $\hat{a}=g(q,S)$ denote the generated answer and $a^*$ the reference. Quality is measured by token-level F1, $F_1(\hat{a},a^*)$, with exact match reported in text only as a secondary check. Cost $C(q,S)$ is the total tokens of the model call, input plus output at provider tokenisation. The cost-aware objective is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:objective}
J(S) \;=\; F_1(\hat{a}, a^*) \;-\; \lambda\, C(q,S),\qquad \lambda > 0,
\end{equation}
with $\lambda$ left implicit and replaced operationally by the Pareto presentation in Section~\ref{subsec:main_results}, so the recommended threshold is re-selectable for deployments with different relative weights. A strategy is \emph{quality-preserving} if its mean F1 is within $\pm 0.05$ of the fixed $k{=}10$ reference on the same dataset.

Figure~\ref{fig:gold_vs_distractor} illustrates the selection difficulty over $D$: a gold paragraph and a BM25-retrieved distractor for the same HotpotQA question share entities, vocabulary, and discourse style, yielding close embedding scores and motivating the selection rules of Section~\ref{sec:methods}.

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

Five strategies are evaluated, ordered by selector-level structure: none (fixed budget), similarity gap (Adaptive-$k$), per-arm reward statistics (UCB1-TUNED), contextual features (LinUCB), and dense paragraph-level similarity (Noise-Gate, proposed). Each subsection states the design hypothesis, then defines the rule formally. Within the comparator ladder of Section~\ref{sec:introduction}, Adaptive-$k$ is training-free while UCB1-TUNED and LinUCB are learned; Noise-Gate replaces the ladder with a deterministic threshold on a single richer signal.

\subsection{Fixed-$k$ Baselines}
\label{subsec:methods_fixed}

The baselines pass the top $k$ paragraphs ranked by the upstream retrieval score and supply the quality and cost reference points. Four anchors are used on HotpotQA distractor under the configuration of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}: $k{=}0$ at F1 0.387 and 103.5 mean tokens; $k{=}3$ at F1 0.651 and 471.3 mean tokens; $k{=}5$ at F1 0.716 and 731.7 mean tokens; and $k{=}10$ at F1 0.792 and 1452.5 mean tokens. Throughout the paper, $k{=}10$ serves as the quality reference for the $\pm 0.05$ tolerance band, and $k{=}0$ as the parametric-knowledge floor below which selection is uninformative. Each additional pair of paragraphs costs roughly $300$ extra tokens for a smaller F1 increment, so the curve exhibits diminishing returns within the fixed-$k$ family before any adaptive policy is introduced.

\subsection{Adaptive-$k$}
\label{subsec:methods_adaptivek}

Adaptive-$k$~\cite{taguchi2025adaptivek} assumes that the largest gap in sorted similarity scores marks the boundary between relevant and irrelevant context, so a single largest-gap rule replaces a tuned integer $k$. The rule is single-pass, training-free, and parameter-light, adapting the budget to the per-query similarity profile at the deployment cost of a fixed-$k$ baseline. Given scores $s_1 \ge s_2 \ge \cdots \ge s_n$, the cut point of maximum adjacent separation is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:adaptivek}
k^* \;=\; \arg\max_{1\le i<n}(s_i - s_{i+1}).
\end{equation}
A minimum-gap floor $\delta=0.08$ guards against degenerate splits: if $\max_i (s_i - s_{i+1}) < \delta$, the rule falls back to $k=\min(5,n)$. The rule exploits a single structural feature, the rank of similarity scores, and assumes the largest adjacent drop separates evidence from noise.

\subsection{UCB1-TUNED}
\label{subsec:methods_ucb1}

UCB1-TUNED~\cite{auer2002ucb1} assumes that per-arm reward statistics estimated on a training split, with variance-aware exploration, rank arms more accurately than a static rule at lighter cost than full supervision. The bandit reuses the upstream retrieval pool but reorders it by a learned utility with an exploration term that adapts to per-arm variance.

Each paragraph title is an arm; the binary reward is 1 if the title is in the supporting-fact set, 0 otherwise. Per-title means $\mu_a$ and variances $\sigma_a^2$ are estimated online with Welford's recursion over the HotpotQA training split ($n{=}90{,}447$), giving constant storage per arm. At inference, each candidate title $a$ is scored as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ucb1tuned}
\mathrm{score}(a) \;=\; \mu_a \;+\; \sqrt{\frac{\ln T}{n_a}\,\min\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4},\,\sigma_a^2\bigr)},
\end{equation}
with $T$ the cumulative round count and $n_a$ the pull count of arm $a$. The top-$k$ titles are kept; titles unseen during training fall back to the upstream BM25 ordering. The variance-aware exploration term distinguishes UCB1-TUNED from UCB1: arms with low observed variance receive a smaller bonus, so titles with stable rewards rise faster in the ranking.

\subsection{LinUCB}
\label{subsec:methods_linucb}

LinUCB~\cite{chu2011linucb} parameterises reward as a linear function of context features, generalising beyond the training-time arm vocabulary and addressing the cold-start regime of UCB1-TUNED.

Each question--paragraph pair is mapped to a feature vector,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linucb_features}
x(q,d) = [f_1,\, f_2,\, f_3]^\top,\quad \hat{\theta} = A^{-1}b,\quad A = I + \sum_{t=1}^{T} x_t x_t^\top,\quad b = \sum_{t=1}^{T} r_t x_t,
\end{equation}
where $f_1$ is the title-in-question fraction, $f_2$ the normalised question length, and $f_3$ the normalised section length. The three features are constant-time per pair and require no dense embedding inference, the scale constraint motivating a contextual bandit over paragraph-embedding models. The ridge update accumulates $\hat{\theta}$ over training observations $\{(x_t,r_t)\}_{t=1}^{T}$ with binary reward $r_t\in\{0,1\}$. At inference, each candidate is scored by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linucb_score}
p(q,d) \;=\; \hat{\theta}^\top x(q,d) \;+\; \alpha\,\sqrt{x(q,d)^\top A^{-1}\, x(q,d)},
\end{equation}
with the second term the LinUCB exploration bonus controlled by $\alpha$; the top-$k$ paragraphs by $p(q,d)$ are kept.

\subsection{Noise-Gate}
\label{subsec:methods_noisegate}

Noise-Gate assumes that dense paragraph-level cosine similarity to the question discriminates gold evidence from BM25-adjacent distractors when surface and rank features cannot, and that a subsequent redundancy filter removes near-duplicates introduced by paragraph-level granularity. The policy requires no training, runs on a small encoder cached offline, and exposes a single primary tunable parameter, the cosine threshold $\tau$, with $\rho$ retained as a secondary redundancy guard. The two stages are kept separate to address distinct failure modes: the relevance stage blocks low-similarity distractors, and the redundancy stage prevents near-duplicates from accumulating once the relevance threshold is cleared. The procedure is applied to the pool $D$, with embeddings $e_q$ and $\{e_i\}$ computed on a small sentence encoder and cached per dataset.

\textbf{Stage 1 (relevance).} A paragraph is retained if its cosine similarity to the question clears $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cos}
\cos(q, d_i) \;=\; \frac{e_q \cdot e_i}{\|e_q\|\,\|e_i\|} \;\ge\; \tau.
\end{equation}

\textbf{Stage 2 (redundancy).} Among paragraphs surviving Stage 1, $d_i$ is added to $S$ only if its maximum Jaccard overlap with already-selected paragraphs is at most $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:jaccard}
\max_{d_j \in S}\;\frac{|d_i \cap d_j|}{|d_i \cup d_j|} \;\le\; \rho.
\end{equation}

The gate operates on precomputed embeddings without per-dataset training. Algorithm~\ref{alg:noisegate} states the selection in pseudocode.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Noise-Gate context selection.}
\label{alg:noisegate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Question $q$, paragraph pool $D=(d_1,\dots,d_n)$, thresholds $\tau$ and $\rho$
\State Compute embedding $e_q$ for $q$
\State Compute embedding $e_i$ for each $d_i \in D$
\State Rank paragraphs by $\cos(e_q, e_i)$ in descending order
\State $S \leftarrow [\,]$
\For{each paragraph $d_i$ in ranked order}
    \If{$\cos(e_q, e_i) < \tau$}
        \State \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \If{$\max_{d_j \in S} \mathrm{Jaccard}(d_i, d_j) > \rho$}
        \State \textbf{continue}
    \EndIf
    \State append $d_i$ to $S$
\EndFor
\State \Return $S$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation compares Noise-Gate to the baselines and comparators of Section~\ref{sec:methods} under matched samples and a fixed generation model. Section~\ref{subsec:setup} fixes the evaluation harness, Section~\ref{subsec:main_results} reports the unified comparison on HotpotQA distractor, Section~\ref{subsec:transfer_pilots} the transfer pilots on 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue-Ans, and Section~\ref{subsec:threshold_ablation} the joint $(\tau,\rho)$ sweep on HotpotQA.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:setup}

\paragraph{Generation model.} All experiments use a single generation model, \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini}, selected as the cost-effective tier from a major commercial provider so that token-cost reductions translate directly to deployment savings rather than to frontier-model overhead. Fixing the model controls for tokenisation, pricing, and provider-specific prompt behaviour and isolates context selection as the experimental variable.

\paragraph{Embedding model.} Cosine scores are computed on \texttt{multi-qa-MiniLM-L6-cos-v1} (384 dimensions), pre-trained on QA corpora; embeddings are precomputed for the full candidate pool and cached, so the selection cost at inference is dominated by the pairwise cosine evaluation rather than by encoder forward passes.

\paragraph{Datasets and samples.} HotpotQA distractor~\cite{yang2018hotpotqa} is the reference setting, evaluated with a fixed random sample of $n{=}400$ examples from the validation split, large enough to stabilise mean F1 and exact match while remaining practical for matched-sample ablations. 2WikiMultiHopQA~\cite{ho-etal-2020-constructing} is the closest structural transfer test, sharing the titled-paragraph layout and BM25 distractor format of HotpotQA, evaluated at $n{=}100$. MuSiQue-Ans~\cite{trivedi-etal-2022-musique} is the semantic-generalisation test; its nested context is flattened to titled paragraphs before selection, evaluated at $n{=}100$.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Token-level F1 is the primary quality metric; exact match is reported in text only. Mean total tokens per example and mean per-query USD cost on \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini} are the cost metrics. A strategy is quality-preserving if its F1 is within $\pm 0.05$ of fixed $k{=}10$ on the same dataset.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.} For the main threshold sweep, the proposed method uses $\tau\in\{0.20,\,0.25,\,0.30,\,0.35,\,0.50\}$ at $\rho{=}0.65$; the recommended HotpotQA operating point is $(\tau{=}0.30,\,\rho{=}0.65)$. The joint ablation evaluates the same $\tau$ values with $\rho\in\{0.05,\,0.20,\,0.35,\,0.50,\,0.65,\,0.80,\,0.95\}$. The learned strategies (UCB1-TUNED, LinUCB) are trained per-dataset on the corresponding training split; the fixed baselines, Adaptive-$k$, and Noise-Gate are applied unchanged across datasets. Sample sizes are kept matched within a dataset across strategies, so each row of Table~\ref{tab:main_results} is computed on the same examples and the comparison is paired rather than population-level.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{subsec:main_results}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the unified comparison on HotpotQA distractor and the two transfer benchmarks. Noise-Gate at $\tau{=}0.30$ reaches token-level F1 0.764 at 1065.3 mean tokens, a $-26.7\%$ token reduction relative to fixed $k{=}10$ (F1 0.792 at 1452.5 mean tokens) and within the $\pm 0.05$ tolerance band of the reference. The looser setting $\tau{=}0.20$ remains in the band at higher quality (F1 0.779) but saves only $9.3\%$ of tokens, which is why $\tau{=}0.30$ is the row recommended for deployment. The position of $\tau{=}0.30$ on the unified table is the empirical answer to RQ1: a training-free filter does reduce token cost on this benchmark without leaving the tolerance band of the fixed-$k{=}10$ reference.

The three learned comparators do not enter the tolerance band on HotpotQA. Adaptive-$k$ reaches F1 0.627 at 483.1 mean tokens, below fixed $k{=}3$ (F1 0.651) and roughly $0.09$ F1 below fixed $k{=}5$, indicating that the largest-gap rule selects shorter contexts than fixed $k{=}3$ but with worse quality. UCB1-TUNED at $k{=}5$ reaches F1 0.704 at 723.0 mean tokens, $0.012$ F1 below the fixed $k{=}5$ baseline at the same retrieval budget, indicating that the bandit reordering does not recover quality over the upstream BM25 ordering. LinUCB at $k{=}5$ reaches F1 0.620 at 686.0 mean tokens, $0.096$ F1 below fixed $k{=}5$, indicating that the linear contextual model degrades quality relative to keeping the BM25 ordering intact. The structural reasons for these gaps are characterised in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} plots the mean-tokens / F1 plane, with the $\pm 0.05$ tolerance band shaded around the fixed $k{=}10$ reference. The connected curve traces the Noise-Gate $\tau$-sweep at $\rho{=}0.65$. Noise-Gate at $\tau{=}0.30$ is the only point that combines a token reduction with quality inside the band; the next-larger threshold ($\tau{=}0.35$) leaves the band at a larger token reduction. The fixed-$k$ anchors lie above and below the band at their respective token costs, confirming that the fixed-$k$ family alone does not span the same trade-off region as the $\tau$-sweep.

\subsection{Transfer Evaluation}
\label{subsec:transfer_pilots}

The 2WikiMultiHopQA rows of Table~\ref{tab:main_results} indicate that Noise-Gate transfers with one threshold step. At $\tau{=}0.25$, F1 rises to 0.662 against the fixed $k{=}10$ reference at 0.617, a $+0.045$ improvement, while mean tokens drop from 1040.1 to 810.9, a $-22.1\%$ reduction. The threshold shifts one step from the HotpotQA-optimal $\tau{=}0.30$ to $\tau{=}0.25$, consistent with 2WikiMultiHopQA's slightly looser cosine distribution at the same evidence rate; the recalibration is a single-step adjustment selected on the held-out pilot sample rather than a retraining of the gate.

The MuSiQue-Ans rows indicate that the HotpotQA threshold transfers directly. At $\tau{=}0.30$, F1 is 0.551 against the fixed $k{=}10$ reference at 0.499, a $+0.052$ improvement, while mean tokens drop from 1342.9 to 1193.2, a $-11.2\%$ reduction. No threshold change is required, even though MuSiQue-Ans's nested context structure differs from HotpotQA's flat paragraph pool: once flattened, the question and paragraph embeddings span a comparable cosine range and the same threshold separates evidence from distractors.

Across the three benchmarks, Noise-Gate matches or exceeds fixed $k{=}10$ at lower token cost with at most one threshold step of recalibration on a small held-out sample. The transfer evidence is the empirical answer to RQ3 within the dataset family considered: the same threshold scheme is reusable across distractor-heavy multi-hop benchmarks with no training pipeline.

\subsection{Threshold Ablations}
\label{subsec:threshold_ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:noise_gate_thresholds} reports the joint $(\tau,\rho)$ ablation on the 395 HotpotQA distractor question IDs present in every ablation cell. At the recommended redundancy threshold $\rho{=}0.65$, increasing $\tau$ from 0.20 to 0.50 reduces mean tokens from 1318.4 to 402.6 while tracing the expected quality--cost frontier. The intermediate settings $\tau{=}0.25$ and $\tau{=}0.30$ have the same rounded F1 (0.763), but $\tau{=}0.30$ uses 144.3 fewer tokens per query. Moving one step further to $\tau{=}0.35$ saves an additional 177.4 tokens but drops F1 by 0.034. The $\tau{=}0.30$ row is therefore the Pareto knee, retaining the F1 of $\tau{=}0.25$ at substantially lower token cost without crossing the quality drop observed at $\tau{=}0.35$.

The Jaccard threshold $\rho$ is a secondary control once the overly strict regime is avoided. Across $\rho\ge 0.35$, fixed-$\tau$ F1 varies by at most 0.023 and mean tokens by at most 38.5, far less than the variation induced by changing $\tau$. The Jaccard stage is therefore retained for redundancy control rather than as a primary tuning axis, with $\rho{=}0.65$ used as the operating default because it is the best setting at the recommended $\tau{=}0.30$ and remains in the stable high-$\rho$ region. Practitioners can fix $\rho$ at this value and tune the gate primarily through $\tau$ on a small held-out sample.

An expanded diagnostic sweep on the matched ablation sample clarifies the lower end of the Jaccard range. Table~\ref{tab:jaccard_sensitivity_pilot} fixes $\tau{=}0.30$ and varies $\rho$ from 0.05 to 0.95. Very strict redundancy thresholds remove too much context: $\rho{=}0.05$ reaches F1 0.530 with 257.1 mean tokens, and $\rho{=}0.20$ reaches F1 0.693 with 923.5 mean tokens. For $\rho\ge 0.35$, F1 stays between 0.740 and 0.763 while token counts stabilise between 1037.2 and 1069.5. This pattern supports treating $\rho$ as a redundancy guard rather than the main quality--cost control.

\section{Failure Mode Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section characterises why the three learned comparators underperform Noise-Gate on HotpotQA distractor. Each subsection recalls the design hypothesis stated in Section~\ref{sec:methods}, the appeal of the strategy, and identifies the property of the benchmark that violates it. The pattern across the three subsections is that the signals on which the policies depend are not discriminative on this benchmark, an observation summarised in Table~\ref{tab:failure_matrix}.

\subsection{Adaptive-$k$: similarity gaps land in the wrong cluster}
\label{subsec:fail_adaptivek}

\textbf{Recall the appeal.} Adaptive-$k$ assumes that the largest gap in sorted similarity scores separates relevant context from noise, and replaces a tuned integer $k$ with a single largest-gap rule that adapts the budget to the per-query similarity profile.

\textbf{Why it breaks.} On HotpotQA distractor, BM25 selects distractors precisely because they are topically adjacent to gold evidence; their embedding scores cluster close to those of the supporting facts, and the largest adjacent gap typically falls inside the distractor cluster rather than between distractor and evidence. The numbers reflect this regime: mean $k^*{=}3.02$, mean total tokens 483.1, F1 0.627, below fixed $k{=}3$ (0.651), $k{=}5$ (0.716), and $k{=}10$ (0.792). The cut is compact but in the wrong cluster, so the selected set excludes genuine evidence while retaining topically adjacent but unhelpful context.

\subsection{UCB1-TUNED: title coverage is sparse across train and validation}
\label{subsec:fail_ucb1}

\textbf{Recall the appeal.} UCB1-TUNED assumes that the training-time arm vocabulary covers the inference-time arm vocabulary, so per-arm reward statistics estimated on a training split rank arms accurately at light training cost.

\textbf{Why it breaks.} HotpotQA draws titles from a Wikipedia-scale distribution, so cold-start rates climb steeply with the retrieval budget: $61.5\%$, $75.2\%$, and $83.8\%$ of validation queries at $k\in\{2,3,5\}$ touch arms unseen during training. Most queries are decided by BM25 fallback rather than by the bandit estimate, which collapses the contribution of the bandit machinery itself. At the best operating point ($k{=}5$), UCB1-TUNED reaches F1 0.704 at 723.0 mean tokens, below the matched fixed $k{=}5$ baseline at F1 0.716.

\subsection{LinUCB: lexical features cannot separate crafted distractors}
\label{subsec:fail_linucb}

\textbf{Recall the appeal.} LinUCB addresses the cold-start regime of arm-indexed bandits by scoring documents through a linear model in context features, so previously unseen arms can still be evaluated on the basis of features rather than identity.

\textbf{Why it breaks.} The features tractable at this scale (token overlap, title-in-question fraction, normalised section length) are matched by construction with BM25-retrieved distractors, since BM25 itself selects on a similar lexical surface. The two populations are therefore nearly indistinguishable in the chosen feature space, and a linear reward model cannot separate gold evidence from distractors. Per-$k$ F1 is 0.510, 0.522, and 0.620 at $k\in\{2,3,5\}$, with mean tokens 344, 455, and 686 respectively.

\subsection{Why the noise gate succeeds}
\label{subsec:why_noise_gate}

The three failure mechanisms share a common cause: the signals on which the learned policies depend, namely gap monotonicity, arm identity, and surface-lexical features, are not discriminative on HotpotQA distractor. Dense paragraph-level cosine similarity provides a signal that the other policies do not exploit, and a deterministic threshold avoids both arm-vocabulary cold-start and the misplaced-gap problem. At $(\tau{=}0.30,\,\rho{=}0.65)$, Noise-Gate reaches F1 0.764 at 1065.3 mean tokens, a $-26.7\%$ token reduction against fixed $k{=}10$, while the closest learned operating point (UCB1-TUNED at $k{=}5$) reaches F1 0.704 at 723.0 mean tokens but with $83.8\%$ cold-start. Table~\ref{tab:failure_matrix} summarises the correspondence between the design hypothesis, the structural mismatch, and the observed F1.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Pareto interpretation.} The recommended operating point $\tau{=}0.30$ corresponds to the Pareto knee in Table~\ref{tab:noise_gate_thresholds} and Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}: nearly identical F1 to $\tau{=}0.25$ at substantially lower token cost. The looser setting $\tau{=}0.25$ remains within the $\pm 0.05$ tolerance band as a quality-leaning alternative when tighter quality is preferred to maximising token reduction. The expanded Jaccard sweep in Table~\ref{tab:jaccard_sensitivity_pilot} supports the same interpretation: once $\rho$ is at least 0.35, the redundancy stage is comparatively stable, while overly strict thresholds delete useful evidence.

\paragraph{Cost implications at scale.} On \texttt{gpt-5.4-mini}, the per-query cost drops from $2.95\times 10^{-4}$ USD at fixed $k{=}10$ to $2.17\times 10^{-4}$ USD at $\tau{=}0.30$. At one million queries per day, the annualised saving is approximately USD 28{,}000. The embedding step is precomputed and cached, so the saving is realised against a one-off indexing cost rather than recurring inference overhead.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Four limitations qualify the present findings. First, transfer evidence comes from $n{=}100$ pilots on two further datasets rather than from full validation splits. Second, a single generation model is fixed; stronger models may tolerate noisier context and shift the optimal threshold. Third, the recommended threshold is dataset-specific: calibration is required per domain on a small held-out sample. Fourth, the bandit comparators are evaluated on HotpotQA-style benchmarks; closed-vocabulary settings may admit different conclusions.

\paragraph{Future work.} Promising directions include a two-stage hybrid combining the gate with a lightweight cross-encoder, an adaptive $\tau$ chosen from the per-query similarity distribution, full-scale evaluation on 2WikiMultiHopQA and MuSiQue-Ans together with open-domain benchmarks such as FEVER and Natural Questions, and replication of the present comparison on stronger generation models.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A training-free two-stage filter that combines a cosine-similarity threshold $\tau$ with a Jaccard-overlap redundancy threshold $\rho$ reaches token-level F1 0.764 at 1065.3 mean tokens on HotpotQA distractor, within the $\pm 0.05$ tolerance band of fixed $k{=}10$ and with a 26.7\% token reduction. The threshold ablations indicate that $\tau$ controls the main quality--cost frontier, while $\rho$ is stable once it avoids the overly strict regime below 0.35. The three learned comparators (Adaptive-$k$, UCB1-TUNED, LinUCB) underperform on this class of benchmarks because the signals they exploit, namely gap monotonicity, arm identity, and surface-lexical features, are not discriminative on distractor-heavy multi-hop QA. For this class of benchmarks, deterministic threshold filtering on dense embeddings is the strongest test-time strategy observed, with thresholds calibratable on a small held-out sample and no training pipeline.
